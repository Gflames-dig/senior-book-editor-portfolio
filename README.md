# MY APPROACH TO BOOK EDITING

I believe an editor should understand what the author is trying to say before deciding how something should be changed.

My editing process therefore begins with understanding the manuscript's:

- Purpose
- Message
- Audience
- Narrative
- Tone
- Voice
- Structure

From there, I identify areas where the manuscript can be strengthened.

## Preserving the Author's Context

One of my strongest editorial principles is preserving the author's original context.

When I rewrite a sentence or paragraph, I do not simply replace it with something that sounds better to me.

I first ask:

> What is the author actually trying to communicate?

The revision should then communicate that same idea more clearly, naturally, and effectively.

## Substantial Rewriting

Some of the books I have edited have required extensive rewriting.

At times, the editing process has felt almost like rewriting the entire manuscript because of the amount of language refinement required.

However, the purpose of that rewriting has always been to strengthen the author's original work rather than replace the author's ideas.

I preserve:

- The original message
- The author's intended meaning
- The underlying story
- Important personal experiences
- The author's perspective
- The intended emotional effect

while improving:

- Grammar
- Expression
- Flow
- Structure
- Tone
- Consistency
- Readability
- Overall presentation

# MANUSCRIPT EDITING DEMONSTRATION

The following demonstration illustrates the type of sentence-level work I apply during manuscript editing.

## Before

> Mara had always wanted to leave the town, but the town was the only place she had ever known and this made the decision difficult for her. Every morning she walked past the old station, thinking about the trains and the different places that they went, although she never actually went inside the station because she was afraid that if she did she would have to make a decision.

## After

> Mara had always wanted to leave town. Yet it was the only place she had ever known, which made the decision harder than she expected. Each morning, she passed the old station and watched the trains carry other people toward unfamiliar places. She never went inside. Part of her feared that once she did, she would have to choose.

## Editorial Reasoning

The revision:

- Removes unnecessary repetition
- Improves sentence structure
- Strengthens pacing
- Improves readability
- Preserves the central meaning
- Maintains the reflective tone
- Creates stronger narrative movement

The objective is not to make the writing sound like the editor.

The objective is to make the author's writing clearer and stronger while preserving its identity.

# LINE EDITING AND COPYEDITING DEMONSTRATION

## Before

> The committee have reviewed the manuscript carefully, however there is still several sections which needs further clarification. In addition, the authors argument is not always clear and some examples appears to be repeated unnecessarily.

## After

> The committee has reviewed the manuscript carefully; however, several sections still require clarification. In addition, the author's argument is not always clear, and some examples appear to be unnecessarily repeated.

## Corrections

| Original | Correction | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| committee have | committee has | Subject-verb agreement |
| however there | however, several | Punctuation and structure |
| there is several sections | several sections | Agreement and concision |
| authors argument | author's argument | Possessive apostrophe |
| examples appears | examples appear | Subject-verb agreement |
| repeated unnecessarily | unnecessarily repeated | Improved phrasing |

# EDITORIAL FEEDBACK

Effective editorial feedback should not simply identify what is wrong.

It should explain:

1. What the issue is
2. Why it matters
3. How it can be improved

## Sample Editorial Feedback

> The chapter has a strong emotional premise and a clear sense of place. The main opportunity is to make the protagonist's objective more visible earlier.
>
> At present, the reader understands what the character feels before understanding what the character intends to do. Consider giving the scene a concrete decision, obstacle, or immediate task within the opening pages.
>
> This would preserve the reflective tone while adding forward momentum. The descriptive passages could also be reviewed to determine which details deepen the atmosphere and which interrupt the progression of the scene.
>
> The underlying tension should be retained. The objective is not to replace the author's style, but to make the narrative direction clearer and the reading experience more engaging.

## My Editorial Feedback Principles

My feedback is:

- Specific
- Constructive
- Respectful
- Actionable
- Clear
- Focused on the manuscript
- Sensitive to authorial voice

# AI-GENERATED EDITORIAL QUALITY REVIEW

My experience with language-focused AI work also allows me to evaluate AI-generated writing from an editorial perspective.

## AI-Generated Sample

> The novel is a powerful book that talks about many important themes and it is very interesting because the author uses a lot of good descriptions, which makes the reader to understand the story better and also makes it more enjoyable.

## Editorial Assessment

### Grammar

"makes the reader to understand" is not idiomatic English in this construction.

A better construction is:

> "helps the reader understand"

### Precision

Expressions such as:

- powerful book
- important themes
- very interesting
- good descriptions

are too general to provide useful editorial analysis.

### Sentence Structure

The sentence contains several connected ideas and would benefit from tighter construction.

### Edited Version

> The novel explores several significant themes through vivid description and carefully developed scenes, helping readers understand the story while deepening its emotional impact.

## AI Editorial Review Criteria

When reviewing AI-generated content, I consider:

- Grammar
- Syntax
- Clarity
- Precision
- Relevance
- Tone
- Structure
- Repetition
- Instruction adherence
- Consistency
- Overall usefulness
